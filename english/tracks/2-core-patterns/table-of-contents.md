# Core English Patterns — Table of Contents

**56 lessons · 112 patterns · Pre-A1 → B1.** This is the grammar-and-function spine for the English program. Each lesson teaches two patterns that naturally work together in one exchange.

Every pattern is introduced through a complete, ordinary sentence. The frame—not the terminology—is the content to retrieve. Later examples use language already taught wherever possible.

> **One lesson = two model sentences + two reusable patterns.** Meaning and recognition come first; guided and personal production follow. Contextual English may recycle a pattern only after its Core prerequisite.

| Stage | Lessons | Working level | Learner outcome |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Foundation | 1–13 | Pre-A1 → A1 | make basic statements and questions about self and routines |
| Everyday independence | 14–35 | A1 → A2 | handle quantities, places, stories, plans, needs, and comparisons |
| Connected conversation | 36–49 | A2 → early B1 | discuss experience, reasons, conditions, requests, and hypotheticals |
| Independent use | 50–56 | B1 | tell layered stories, infer, negotiate tone, and discuss familiar work topics |

## Unit 1 · First conversations — identity, objects, and place · 6 lessons

**1. Hi, I’m Mina — introduce and confirm**
- “I’m Mina.” — `I am / I'm + name or identity`
- “Are you Alex?” — `Are you + name or identity?`

**2. I’m from Seoul — people and origin**
- “I’m from Seoul.” — `subject pronoun + be + from + place`
- “Her name is Sara.” — `possessive adjective + noun`

**3. I’m a designer — one and more than one**
- “I’m a designer.” — `a/an + singular count noun`
- “We’re designers.” — `plural noun + are`

**4. This is my bag — point out and own**
- “This is my bag. Those are your keys.” — `this/that/these/those + be`
- “It’s mine.” — `possessive pronoun: mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs`

**5. Who is that? — ask for basic facts**
- “Who is that?” — `who/what + be + ...?`
- “How old is your son?” — `how old + be + person?`

**6. There’s a café near here — say what exists and where**
- “There’s a café near here.” — `there is/there are`
- “It’s next to the bank.” — `be + preposition of place`

*▸ Checkpoint 1: introduce two people, identify four objects, and place three things on a simple map.*

## Unit 2 · Everyday actions — routines and actions now · 7 lessons

**7. I work from home — routine actions**
- “I work from home.” — `subject + base verb`
- “We have lunch at twelve.” — `have + routine noun phrase`

**8. I don’t drive — present negatives**
- “I don’t drive.” — `I/you/we/they + don't + base verb`
- “She doesn’t drink coffee.” — `he/she/it + doesn't + base verb`

**9. Do you cook? — present questions**
- “Do you cook?” — `do/does + subject + base verb?`
- “Where do you work?” — `wh-word + do/does + subject + base verb?`

**10. She starts at nine — third-person habits**
- “She starts at nine.” — `he/she/it + verb-s`
- “He goes to work by bus.” — common third-person forms `goes/does/has`

**11. I usually walk — frequency**
- “I usually walk to work.” — `subject + frequency adverb + action verb`
- “I’m often tired after work.” — `be + frequency adverb`

**12. Open the app — instructions and shared action**
- “Open the app and tap ‘Start.’” — `imperative + and + imperative`
- “Let’s try it together.” — `let's + base verb`

**13. I’m waiting outside — action now**
- “I’m waiting outside.” — `be + verb-ing`
- “I usually drive, but I’m taking the train today.” — `present simple + but + present continuous`

*▸ Checkpoint 2: describe a normal weekday, ask a partner three questions, then explain what is different today.*

## Unit 3 · Getting things done — quantity, time, and movement · 6 lessons

**14. How much is it? — prices and numbers**
- “How much is this?” — `how much + be + item?`
- “It’s twenty-five dollars.” — `be + number + currency`

**15. We need some milk — count and non-count needs**
- “We need some apples.” — `some + plural count noun`
- “We don’t have any milk.” — `any + plural/non-count noun in negatives and questions`

**16. How many do we need? — ask about quantity**
- “How many eggs do we need?” — `how many + plural noun?`
- “How much rice do we need?” — `how much + non-count noun?`

**17. A bottle of water — package and portion**
- “Can I have a bottle of water?” — `container/measure + of + non-count noun`
- “I’d like two cups of coffee.” — `number + container + of + noun`

**18. On Friday at three — dates and appointments**
- “Let’s meet on Friday at three.” — `on + day/date; at + clock time`
- “I’m on vacation in August.” — `in + month/year/season`

**19. How do I get there? — route and duration**
- “Go straight and turn left at the light.” — `direction imperative + at + landmark`
- “It takes about twenty minutes by bus.” — `it takes + duration + by + transport`

*▸ Checkpoint 3: buy three items, arrange a meeting, and guide someone from a station to a café.*

## Unit 4 · What happened — past events and stories · 5 lessons

**20. I was tired — past states**
- “I was tired yesterday.” — `was/were + adjective or noun`
- “There were a lot of people.” — `there was/there were`

**21. We watched a movie — completed actions**
- “We watched a movie.” — `regular verb + -ed`
- “We went out for dinner.” — frequent irregular past forms

**22. Did you have fun? — past questions and negatives**
- “Did you have fun?” — `did + subject + base verb?`
- “I didn’t stay late.” — `didn't + base verb`

**23. First we ate, then we walked — sequence a short story**
- “First we ate, and then we walked home.” — `first/then/finally + past event`
- “I saw her two days ago.” — `past event + last.../...ago`

**24. I was cooking when you called — background and interruption**
- “I was cooking when you called.” — `past continuous + when + past simple`
- “While I was cooking, he was setting the table.” — `while + past continuous, past continuous`

*▸ Checkpoint 4: ask about a weekend and retell it with a setting, three events, and a reaction.*

## Unit 5 · Wants, plans, and limits — modal meaning · 6 lessons

**25. I want to go — wants and polite choices**
- “I want to visit New York.” — `want to + base verb`
- “I’d like to book a room.” — `would like to + base verb / would like + noun`

**26. I can swim — ability and practical requests**
- “I can swim, but I can’t dive.” — `can/can't + base verb`
- “Can you help me?” — `can/could + person + base verb?`

**27. We’re going to leave early — intentions and arrangements**
- “We’re going to leave early.” — `be going to + base verb`
- “I’m meeting Sam at six.” — `present continuous + future time`

**28. I’ll call you — decisions and possibility**
- “I’ll call you tonight.” — `will + base verb` for a decision or promise
- “I might be late.” — `might/may + base verb` for possibility

**29. You should rest — advice and need**
- “You should rest.” — `should/shouldn't + base verb`
- “You need to see a doctor.” — `need to + base verb`

**30. You have to show ID — obligation and freedom**
- “You have to show ID.” — `have to/must + base verb`
- “You don’t have to print it.” — `don't have to + base verb` for no obligation

*▸ Checkpoint 5: make a plan, ask for help, give advice, and explain one rule plus one optional action.*

## Unit 6 · Describe and choose — degree and comparison · 5 lessons

**31. It’s really comfortable — description and intensity**
- “It’s a comfortable black chair.” — `opinion/size/age/color + noun`
- “It’s really comfortable.” — `really/very/a little + adjective`

**32. It’s too small — limits and sufficiency**
- “It’s too small for me.” — `too + adjective + for + person`
- “It isn’t big enough.” — `adjective + enough / enough + noun`

**33. This one is cheaper — compare two things**
- “This one is cheaper than that one.” — `comparative + than`
- “The rooms are as quiet as each other.” — `as + adjective + as`

**34. It’s the best option — compare a group and choose**
- “It’s the best option in this area.” — `the + superlative + in/of`
- “I prefer the blue one to the gray one.” — `prefer + noun + to + noun`

**35. I’m bored; the movie is boring — feelings and impressions**
- “I’m bored, but the movie isn’t boring.” — `person + be + -ed; cause + be + -ing`
- “You look tired. This soup tastes great.” — `look/sound/taste/feel + adjective`

*▸ Checkpoint 6: compare three options, reject one with a reason, and recommend the best choice.*

## Unit 7 · Connect time and ideas — experience, duration, and reasons · 7 lessons

**36. Have you ever tried it? — life experience**
- “Have you ever tried surfing?” — `have/has + subject + ever + past participle?`
- “I’ve never tried it.” — `have/has never + past participle`

**37. I’ve just finished — recent result**
- “I’ve just finished the report.” — `have/has just + past participle`
- “Have you sent it yet? Yes, I’ve already sent it.” — `yet` in questions/negatives; `already` in positives

**38. I went last year — experience vs finished time**
- “I’ve been to Spain.” — `present perfect` with no finished time
- “I went there last year.” — `past simple + finished time`

**39. I’ve lived here for five years — duration**
- “I’ve lived here for five years.” — `present perfect + for/since`
- “I’ve been working all morning.” — `have/has been + verb-ing`

**40. If it rains, we’ll stay home — real conditions**
- “If it rains, we’ll stay home.” — `if + present, will + base verb`
- “Unless it rains, we’ll go.” — `unless + present, will + base verb`

**41. I stayed home because I was sick — reason and contrast**
- “I was sick, so I stayed home.” — `because + reason / so + result`
- “Although I was tired, I finished it.” — `although + clause / but + contrasting clause`

**42. The person who called — identify people, things, and places**
- “She’s the person who called me.” — `person + who + clause`
- “That’s the café where we met.” — `thing + that + clause / place + where + clause`

*▸ Checkpoint 7: discuss an experience, explain how long it has continued, and make a conditional plan with reasons.*

## Unit 8 · Keep interaction smooth — politeness, reports, and hypotheticals · 7 lessons

**43. Would you mind helping me? — graded requests**
- “Could you open the window?” — `could/would + you + base verb?`
- “Would you mind helping me?” — `would you mind + verb-ing?`

**44. Do you know where it is? — indirect questions**
- “Do you know where it is?” — `question opener + wh-clause in statement order`
- “I was wondering if you had a table.” — `I was wondering if/whether + clause`

**45. Turn it on; look after it — phrasal-verb patterns**
- “Turn the light on. Turn it on.” — separable verb + particle; pronoun in the middle
- “Can you look after my bag?” — inseparable verb + particle + object

**46. It’s made here — focus on process or result**
- “These bags are made locally.” — `am/is/are + past participle`
- “My phone was stolen yesterday.” — `was/were + past participle`

**47. She said she was busy — report another person**
- “She said she was busy.” — `say + reported clause`
- “She told me to wait.” — `tell + person + to + base verb`

**48. If I had more time — imagine and wish**
- “If I had more time, I’d travel more.” — `if + past, would + base verb`
- “I wish I had more time.” — `wish + past form` for a present unreal situation

**49. It’s good, isn’t it? — invite and echo agreement**
- “It’s good, isn’t it?” — `statement + question tag`
- “I love it.” “So do I.” — `so/neither + auxiliary + subject`

*▸ Checkpoint 8: make a polite request, ask an indirect question, report the response, and discuss an imagined alternative.*

## Unit 9 · Independent conversation — layered time, inference, and tone · 7 lessons

**50. The train had left — show the earlier past**
- “The train had left when we arrived.” — `past perfect + when + past simple`
- “By the time we got there, the store had closed.” — `by the time + past simple, past perfect`

**51. He must be busy — infer now**
- “He must be busy.” — `must be` for a strong present deduction
- “He might be in a meeting.” — `might/could be` for a possible present deduction

**52. I should have called — look back at possibilities**
- “I should have called earlier.” — `should have + past participle`
- “I might have left it at home.” — `might/could/must have + past participle`

**53. I had my phone repaired — arrange a service**
- “I had my phone repaired.” — `have + object + past participle`
- “Where can I get this cleaned?” — `get + object + past participle`

**54. The train leaves at six — schedules and future in progress**
- “The train leaves at six tomorrow.” — `present simple + future time` for a timetable
- “This time tomorrow, I’ll be flying home.” — `will be + verb-ing`

**55. You have a point, but — concede and organize**
- “You have a point, but I’m not convinced.” — `concession + but + position`
- “On the one hand..., on the other hand...” — balanced discourse frame

**56. I need help / I was wondering if — choose the register**
- “Can you help me?” / “Could you possibly help me?” — casual-to-polite request scale
- “I don’t agree.” / “I see what you mean, but...” — direct-to-diplomatic disagreement scale

*▸ Final performance: solve a familiar problem through a five-minute conversation that includes a past explanation, an inference, a polite request, a response, and a negotiated next step.*
