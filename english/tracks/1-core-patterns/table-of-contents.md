# Core English Speaking Patterns — Table of Contents

**72 lessons · 144 speaking patterns · Pre-A1 → B1.** This is the main English sequence. Learners do not study a grammar label and then search for a use; they borrow two complete frames, change the slots, and use both in one small conversation.

> **One lesson = one can-do + two literal speaking frames.** Grammar remains in the author-only [`grammar-coverage-map.md`](../../reference/grammar-coverage-map.md). A learner-facing pattern must contain words someone can actually say, not a formula such as “subject + base verb.”

| Stage | Lessons | Working level | Learner outcome |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| First exchanges | 1–24 | Pre-A1 → A1 | exchange basic information and complete simple transactions |
| Everyday independence | 25–42 | A1 → A2 | tell short stories, make plans, explain rules, and compare options |
| Connected conversation | 43–60 | A2 → early B1 | discuss experience, connect reasons, and manage polite interaction |
| Independent use | 61–72 | B1 | imagine, infer, clarify, report, and negotiate familiar problems |

## Unit 1 · Meet and identify people and things · 6 lessons

**1. Hi, I’m Mina — exchange names**

*Can-do: introduce yourself and learn another person’s name.*
- “Hi, I’m Mina.” — `Hi, I'm ___.`
- “What’s your name?” — `What's your name?`

**2. I’m from Seoul — exchange origins**

*Can-do: say where you are from and ask someone else.*
- “I’m from Seoul.” — `I'm from ___.`
- “Where are you from?” — `Where are you from?`

**3. I’m a designer — exchange roles**

*Can-do: say what you do and ask about another person’s role.*
- “I’m a designer.” — `I'm a ___.`
- “What do you do?” — `What do you do?`

**4. This is my friend — introduce someone else**

*Can-do: introduce another person and add one fact about them.*
- “This is my friend, Leo.” — `This is my ___, ___.`
- “He’s from Canada.” — `He's from ___.`

**5. Is this yours? — check ownership**

*Can-do: identify who an object belongs to.*
- “Is this your bag?” — `Is this your ___?`
- “Yes, it’s mine.” — `Yes, it's mine.`

**6. Is there a café nearby? — find what exists**

*Can-do: ask whether a place exists and locate it.*
- “Is there a café near here?” — `Is there a ___ near here?`
- “There’s one next to the bank.” — `There's one next to ___.`

*▸ Checkpoint 1: meet someone, introduce a third person, return an object, and locate one place.*

## Unit 2 · Talk about everyday routines · 6 lessons

**7. I start work at nine — share a routine**

*Can-do: state one regular action and invite another person to share theirs.*
- “I start work at nine.” — `I ___ at ___.`
- “How about you?” — `How about you?`

**8. What time do you get up? — ask about routine times**

*Can-do: ask and answer when a routine happens.*
- “What time do you get up?” — `What time do you ___?`
- “I usually get up at seven.” — `I usually ___ at ___.`

**9. I don’t drive to work — say what is not routine**

*Can-do: contrast something you do with something you do not do.*
- “I don’t drive to work.” — `I don't ___ to work.`
- “I take the bus instead.” — `I ___ instead.`

**10. Does she work nearby? — ask about another person**

*Can-do: exchange routine information about a third person.*
- “Does she work nearby?” — `Does she ___?`
- “Yes, she works downtown.” — `Yes, she works ___.`

**11. How often do you exercise? — talk about frequency**

*Can-do: ask how often something happens and give an approximate answer.*
- “How often do you exercise?” — `How often do you ___?`
- “I exercise two or three times a week.” — `I ___ about ___ times a week.`

**12. I usually drive, but today I’m walking — contrast now and usual**

*Can-do: explain how today differs from your normal routine.*
- “What are you doing today?” — `What are you doing today?`
- “I usually drive, but today I’m walking.” — `I usually ___, but today I'm ___ing.`

*▸ Checkpoint 2: compare a normal weekday with today and ask three follow-up questions.*

## Unit 3 · Handle quantity, time, and movement · 6 lessons

**13. How much is this? — ask and state a price**

*Can-do: check a price and confirm what you heard.*
- “How much is this?” — `How much is this?`
- “Did you say twenty or thirty?” — `Did you say ___ or ___?`

**14. Do we have any milk? — check availability**

*Can-do: ask whether something is available and answer accurately.*
- “Do we have any milk?” — `Do we have any ___?`
- “We have some apples, but we don’t have any milk.” — `We have some ___, but we don't have any ___.`

**15. How many do we need? — ask about quantity**

*Can-do: ask for the right quantity of countable or uncountable things.*
- “How many eggs do we need?” — `How many ___ do we need?`
- “How much rice do we need?” — `How much ___ do we need?`

**16. Two cups of coffee, please — order measured items**

*Can-do: request a numbered or measured amount.*
- “Two cups of coffee, please.” — `___ cups of ___, please.`
- “Anything else?” — `Anything else?`

**17. Are you free on Friday? — arrange a time**

*Can-do: check availability and suggest an appointment time.*
- “Are you free on Friday afternoon?” — `Are you free on ___?`
- “Let’s meet at three.” — `Let's meet at ___.`

**18. How do I get there? — ask for a route and duration**

*Can-do: understand a simple route and how long it takes.*
- “How do I get to the station?” — `How do I get to ___?`
- “Go left at the bank. It takes about ten minutes.” — `Go ___ at ___. It takes about ten minutes.`

*▸ Checkpoint 3: buy several items, arrange a meeting, and follow a route on a map.*

## Unit 4 · Express wants and complete transactions · 6 lessons

**19. I want to see the city — state a goal**

*Can-do: say what you want to do and ask about another person’s goal.*
- “I want to see the city.” — `I want to ___.`
- “What do you want to do?” — `What do you want to do?`

**20. I’d like the chicken — order and choose**

*Can-do: make a polite choice and select between options.*
- “I’d like the chicken, please.” — `I'd like ___, please.`
- “I’ll take the blue one.” — `I'll take the ___ one.`

**21. Can you swim? — exchange abilities**

*Can-do: ask about an ability and qualify your answer.*
- “Can you swim?” — `Can you ___?`
- “I can swim, but I can’t dive.” — `I can ___, but I can't ___.`

**22. Could you help me? — ask someone to act**

*Can-do: make and answer a practical request.*
- “Could you help me with this?” — `Could you help me with ___?`
- “Sure. What do you need?” — `Sure. What do you need?`

**23. Can I try this on? — ask permission**

*Can-do: ask whether an action is allowed and respond.*
- “Can I try this on?” — `Can I ___?`
- “Yes, go ahead.” — `Yes, go ahead.`

**24. Sorry, I didn’t catch that — repair communication**

*Can-do: ask for slower speech or clarification.*
- “Sorry, I didn’t catch that.” — `Sorry, I didn't catch that.`
- “Could you say that again more slowly?” — `Could you say that again more slowly?`

*▸ Checkpoint 4: complete a purchase, ask permission, request help, and repair one misunderstanding.*

## Unit 5 · Tell what happened · 6 lessons

**25. I was tired yesterday — describe a past state**

*Can-do: say how a person or place was in the past.*
- “I was tired yesterday.” — `I was ___ yesterday.`
- “There were a lot of people.” — `There were ___ at ___.`

**26. We watched a movie — report a completed action**

*Can-do: state one completed event and add when it happened.*
- “We watched a movie last night.” — `We ___ed ___ last night.`
- “What did you do?” — `What did you do ___?`

**27. We went out for dinner — use common past actions**

*Can-do: tell a short account using frequent irregular past verbs.*
- “We went to the market and had lunch.” — `We went to ___ and had ___.`
- “Then I bought a souvenir and came home.” — `Then I bought ___ and came home.`

**28. Did you have fun? — ask and deny a past action**

*Can-do: ask whether something happened and give a negative answer.*
- “Did you have fun?” — `Did you ___?`
- “I didn’t stay late, but I called a friend.” — `I didn't ___, but I ___ed.`

**29. First we ate, then we walked — sequence events**

*Can-do: put three completed events in a clear order.*
- “First we ate, and then we walked around.” — `First we ___, and then we ___.`
- “In the end, we took a taxi home.” — `In the end, we ___.`

**30. I was cooking when you called — add background**

*Can-do: explain what was in progress when another event happened.*
- “I was cooking when you called.” — `I was ___ing when you ___ed.`
- “While I was cooking, he was setting the table.” — `While I was ___ing, he was ___ing.`

*▸ Checkpoint 5: ask about a weekend and retell it with a state, sequence, background, and interruption.*

## Unit 6 · Make plans and explain limits · 6 lessons

**31. I’m going to visit my family — state an intention**

*Can-do: exchange intended future activities.*
- “I’m going to visit my family.” — `I'm going to ___.`
- “What are you going to do?” — `What are you going to do ___?`

**32. I’m meeting Sam at six — confirm an arrangement**

*Can-do: state and check a fixed personal arrangement.*
- “I’m meeting Sam at six.” — `I'm meeting ___ at ___.`
- “Are you still coming on Tuesday?” — `Are you still ___ing on ___?`

**33. I’ll call you tonight — decide and promise**

*Can-do: make an immediate decision and a promise.*
- “I’ll help you.” — `I'll ___.`
- “Don’t worry. I’ll send it by noon.” — `Don't worry. I'll ___ by ___.`

**34. I might be late — express possibility**

*Can-do: say what may happen and respond with a contingency.*
- “I might be late.” — `I might ___.`
- “If you do, I’ll wait outside.” — `If you do, I'll ___.`

**35. You should get some rest — advise and strengthen advice**

*Can-do: give ordinary advice and explain when action is necessary.*
- “You should get some rest.” — `You should ___.`
- “If it gets worse, you need to see a doctor.” — `If it gets worse, you need to ___.`

**36. You have to show ID — explain rules and freedom**

*Can-do: distinguish required, optional, and prohibited actions.*
- “You have to sign it, but you don’t have to print it.” — `You have to ___, but you don't have to ___.`
- “You mustn’t smoke here.” — `You mustn't ___.`

*▸ Checkpoint 6: make a plan, warn about uncertainty, give advice, and explain three kinds of rules.*

## Unit 7 · Describe, compare, and choose · 6 lessons

**37. It’s a comfortable black chair — describe an item**

*Can-do: identify an item using two useful attributes.*
- “It’s a comfortable chair.” — `It's a ___ ___.`
- “I’m looking for something quieter.” — `I'm looking for something ___.`

**38. It’s really comfortable — express degree**

*Can-do: strengthen or soften a description.*
- “It’s really comfortable.” — `It's really ___.`
- “It’s a little small, but it’s comfortable.” — `It's a little ___, but ___.`

**39. It’s too small — explain a limit**

*Can-do: reject an option because it exceeds or fails a limit.*
- “It’s too expensive for me.” — `It's too ___ for me.`
- “It isn’t big enough.” — `It isn't ___ enough.`

**40. This one is cheaper — compare two options**

*Can-do: compare two options on two dimensions.*
- “This one is cheaper than that one.” — `This one is ___er than that one.`
- “It’s more convenient, but it’s less quiet.” — `It's more ___, but it's less ___.`

**41. It’s the best option — choose from a group**

*Can-do: identify the strongest option and state a preference.*
- “It’s the cheapest option.” — `It's the ___est option.`
- “I prefer the blue one to the gray one.” — `I prefer ___ to ___.`

**42. I’m bored; it’s boring — separate feeling and cause**

*Can-do: describe a reaction and the impression that caused it.*
- “I’m bored because it’s boring.” — `I'm ___ed because it's ___ing.`
- “You look tired.” — `You look ___.`

*▸ Checkpoint 7: compare three options, reject one, and recommend the best one with a personal reaction.*

## Unit 8 · Discuss experience and duration · 6 lessons

**43. Have you ever tried surfing? — ask about experience**

*Can-do: ask whether someone has had an experience and answer negatively.*
- “Have you ever tried surfing?” — `Have you ever ___?`
- “I’ve never tried it, but I’d like to.” — `I've never ___, but I'd like to.`

**44. I’ve just finished — report a recent result**

*Can-do: ask whether something is complete and report its current status.*
- “Have you sent the report yet?” — `Have you ___ yet?`
- “I’ve just sent it.” — `I've just ___.`

**45. I went there last year — add finished time**

*Can-do: move from general experience to one specific occasion.*
- “Have you been to Spain?” — `Have you been to ___?`
- “Yes, I went there last year.” — `Yes, I went there [finished time].`

**46. I’ve lived here for five years — state duration**

*Can-do: say how long a continuing situation has lasted.*
- “How long have you lived here?” — `How long have you ___?`
- “I’ve lived here for five years.” — `I've ___ for ___.`

**47. I’ve been working all morning — explain ongoing activity**

*Can-do: describe an activity continuing over a recent period and its effect.*
- “I’ve been working since nine.” — `I've been ___ing since ___.`
- “That’s why I’m tired.” — `That's why I'm ___.`

**48. We’ve completed three tasks — give an update**

*Can-do: report completed work and what remains.*
- “We’ve completed three of the five tasks.” — `We've completed ___ of ___.`
- “We still need to review the budget.” — `We still need to ___.`

*▸ Checkpoint 8: discuss an experience, tell when it happened, explain its duration, and give a progress update.*

## Unit 9 · Connect reasons, conditions, and descriptions · 6 lessons

**49. I stayed home because I was sick — connect reason and result**

*Can-do: explain why something happened from either direction.*
- “I stayed home because I was sick.” — `I ___ because ___.`
- “I was sick, so I stayed home.” — `___, so I ___.`

**50. I like it, but it’s expensive — add contrast**

*Can-do: recognize a positive point while introducing a limitation.*
- “I like the apartment, but it’s expensive.” — `I like ___, but ___.`
- “Although I was tired, I finished the report.” — `Although ___, I ___.`

**51. If it rains, we’ll stay home — make a real condition**

*Can-do: explain what will happen under a realistic condition.*
- “If it rains, I’ll stay home.” — `If ___, I'll ___.`
- “What will you do if the train is late?” — `What will you do if ___?`

**52. I’ll call when I arrive — use time and negative conditions**

*Can-do: state when something will happen and what could prevent it.*
- “I’ll call you when I arrive.” — `I'll ___ when I ___.`
- “Unless it rains, I’ll walk.” — `Unless ___, I'll ___.`

**53. She’s the person who called — identify precisely**

*Can-do: identify a person or thing by what they do.*
- “She’s the person who called me.” — `She's the person who ___.`
- “It’s the thing that opens the door.” — `It's the thing that ___.`

**54. I went there to meet a client — explain purpose and method**

*Can-do: explain why you acted and recommend a method.*
- “I went there to meet a client.” — `I ___ to ___.`
- “The best way to get there is to take the bus.” — `The best way to ___ is to ___.`

*▸ Checkpoint 9: explain a decision, add a contrast, make a conditional plan, and identify an unknown person or object.*

## Unit 10 · Manage polite and reported interaction · 6 lessons

**55. Would you mind helping me? — grade a request**

*Can-do: make an ordinary or more careful request.*
- “Could you open the window?” — `Could you ___?`
- “Would you mind helping me?” — `Would you mind ___ing?`

**56. Do you mind if I sit here? — ask permission carefully**

*Can-do: request permission and respond without ambiguity.*
- “Do you mind if I sit here?” — `Do you mind if I ___?`
- “Not at all. Go ahead.” — `Not at all. Go ahead.`

**57. Do you know where it is? — ask indirectly**

*Can-do: ask for information without sounding abrupt.*
- “Do you know where the station is?” — `Do you know where ___ is?`
- “Could you tell me when the train leaves?” — `Could you tell me when ___?`

**58. It won’t turn on — troubleshoot a problem**

*Can-do: describe a malfunction and suggest a first check.*
- “My computer won’t start.” — `My ___ won't ___.`
- “Have you tried restarting it?” — `Have you tried ___ing it?`

**59. It’s made locally — focus on a result**

*Can-do: explain how something is produced or what happened to it.*
- “It’s made locally.” — `It's made ___.`
- “My wallet was stolen yesterday.” — `My ___ was ___ yesterday.`

**60. She said she was busy — relay a message**

*Can-do: report information and an instruction from another person.*
- “She said she was busy.” — `She said she was ___.`
- “She told me to wait.” — `She told me to ___.`

*▸ Checkpoint 10: make a careful request, ask an indirect question, solve a malfunction, and relay the response.*

## Unit 11 · Imagine, infer, and look back · 6 lessons

**61. If I had more time, I’d travel — imagine another reality**

*Can-do: discuss what you would do in an unreal present situation.*
- “If I had more time, I’d travel.” — `If I had ___, I'd ___.`
- “What would you do if you won the lottery?” — `What would you do if ___?`

**62. I wish I had more time — express a present wish**

*Can-do: express dissatisfaction with a present situation.*
- “I wish I had a car.” — `I wish I had ___.`
- “I wish I could drive.” — `I wish I could ___.`

**63. He must be busy — infer the present**

*Can-do: distinguish a strong conclusion from a possibility.*
- “He must be busy.” — `He must be ___.`
- “She might be in a meeting.” — `She might be ___.`

**64. I should have called — express regret**

*Can-do: say what would have been a better past action.*
- “I should have called earlier.” — `I should have ___.`
- “I shouldn’t have said that.” — `I shouldn't have ___.`

**65. I might have left it at home — infer the past**

*Can-do: offer possible and strong explanations for a past event.*
- “I might have left it at home.” — `I might have ___.`
- “The traffic must have been bad.” — `It must have ___.`

**66. The train had left when we arrived — order past events**

*Can-do: make clear which of two past events happened first.*
- “The train had already left when we arrived.” — `It had already ___ when we ___.`
- “By the time we arrived, the store had closed.” — `By the time we ___, it had ___.`

*▸ Checkpoint 11: discuss a hypothetical choice, a present wish, a likely explanation, and a past regret.*

## Unit 12 · Handle independent and workplace conversations · 6 lessons

**67. I had my phone repaired — arrange a service**

*Can-do: explain a completed service and ask where to arrange one.*
- “I had my phone repaired.” — `I had my ___ repaired.`
- “Where can I get this cleaned?” — `Where can I get this ___?`

**68. This time tomorrow, I’ll be flying — discuss schedules in progress**

*Can-do: distinguish a timetable from an activity in progress at a future time.*
- “The train leaves at six.” — `The ___ leaves at ___.`
- “This time tomorrow, I’ll be flying home.” — `This time ___, I'll be ___ing.`

**69. You have a point, but — disagree constructively**

*Can-do: acknowledge another view and state a different position.*
- “You have a point, but it’s too expensive.” — `You have a point, but ___.`
- “I see what you mean. However, we need more time.” — `I see what you mean. However, ___.`

**70. What you’re saying is — clarify and summarize**

*Can-do: check a term and restate another person’s meaning.*
- “What do you mean by ‘ready’?” — `What do you mean by ___?`
- “So, what you’re saying is we need more time.” — `So, what you're saying is ___.`

**71. We’re on track, but — report progress and risk**

*Can-do: summarize current progress and identify a possible delay.*
- “We’re on track to finish by Friday.” — `We're on track to ___ by ___.`
- “The main issue is that the supplier is late.” — `The main issue is that ___.`

**72. I was wondering if you could — negotiate a solution**

*Can-do: make a diplomatic request and offer a bounded solution.*
- “I was wondering if you could extend the deadline.” — `I was wondering if you could ___.`
- “I understand your concern; what I can do is send a daily update.” — `I understand ___; what I can do is ___.`

*▸ Final performance: resolve a familiar personal or workplace problem through a five-minute exchange containing clarification, reasons, a diplomatic request, and an agreed next step.*

## Scope boundary

The main spine ends at independent B1 use. Dense idiom sets, literary devices, legal language, advanced inversion, and specialist professional rhetoric belong in later elective tracks rather than this core.
