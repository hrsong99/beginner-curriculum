**Goal:** use known English to hold the floor, react, ask follow-ups, and express a real point of view.

This is the open-ended track. It introduces no grammar syllabus of its own. Each session selects **two conversation moves** from Core and Contextual English, or makes a transparent discourse-level expansion of them, and uses the pair repeatedly around one interesting question.

The same level-sensitive pattern standard applies here; see [`teaching-philosophy.md`](../../reference/teaching-philosophy.md).

> Structure: hook → quick position → Move A → follow-up ladder → Move B → changed/stronger position. Enter by the prerequisite on each topic rather than using one start point for the whole track.

**4 themes · 24 graded launch topics · growing.** The themes are menus, not linear level sequences. Start with any A2 topic whose Core prerequisite has been completed, then move into B1 and B1+ topics. The frames shown are the default pair; tutors may substitute previously learned equivalents.

## Life & Choices · 6 topics

**1. What does success mean to you? [B1+ · Core 70]**
- “For me, success means...” — define a personal idea
- “It’s not only about...; it’s also about...” — broaden a definition

**2. More money or more free time? [B1+ · Core 69]**
- “I prefer... to...” — state a preference
- “You have a point, but...” — consider the cost of the choice

**3. A decision you’re glad you made [B1+ · Core 64]**
- “I decided to... because...” — narrate a decision
- “I should have... sooner.” — evaluate the timing from the present

**4. One habit you want to change [B1 · Core 47]**
- “I’ve been trying to...” — describe an ongoing effort
- “That’s why I’m...” — explain the result of the effort

**5. The perfect day off [B1 · Core 61]**
- “My ideal day would start with...” — build an imagined day
- “After that, I’d probably...” — extend the sequence

**6. Where would you live for a year? [B1 · Core 61]**
- “If I could live anywhere, I’d choose...” — make a hypothetical choice
- “The main reason is that...” — prioritize a reason

## Follow-up ladder for every topic

1. **Fact:** What/when/where?
2. **Reason:** Why? What makes you say that?
3. **Example:** Can you give me an example?
4. **Contrast:** Is that always true? What about the other side?
5. **Hypothetical:** What would change your mind?
6. **Return:** Has your answer changed since the start?

New topics should earn their place by creating genuine curiosity, not by providing a thin excuse to drill a grammar label.
