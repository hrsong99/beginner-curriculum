# English Freetalking — Launch Topics

**Goal:** use known English to hold the floor, react, ask follow-ups, and express a real point of view.

This is the open-ended track. It introduces no grammar syllabus of its own. Each session selects **two conversation moves** from Core and Contextual English and uses them repeatedly around one interesting question.

> Structure: hook → quick position → Move A → follow-up ladder → Move B → changed/stronger position. Recommended from Core Lesson 31; the tutor simplifies the model without simplifying the idea.

**4 themes · 24 launch topics · growing.** The frames shown are the default pair; tutors may substitute previously learned equivalents.

---

## Life & Choices · 6 topics

**1. What does success mean to you?**
- “For me, success means...” — define a personal idea
- “It’s not only about...; it’s also about...” — broaden a definition

**2. More money or more free time?**
- “I’d rather have... because...” — state a preference with a reason
- “On the other hand...” — consider the cost of the choice

**3. A decision you’re glad you made**
- “I decided to... because...” — narrate a decision
- “If I hadn’t..., I might have...” — imagine the alternative

**4. One habit you want to change**
- “I’ve been trying to...” — describe an ongoing effort
- “What makes it difficult is...” — identify the obstacle

**5. The perfect day off**
- “My ideal day would start with...” — build an imagined day
- “After that, I’d probably...” — extend the sequence

**6. Where would you live for a year?**
- “If I could live anywhere, I’d choose...” — make a hypothetical choice
- “The main reason is that...” — prioritize a reason

## Culture & Taste · 6 topics

**1. A movie everyone should see**
- “What I liked most was...” — highlight one feature
- “I’d recommend it to people who...” — qualify a recommendation

**2. A song connected to a memory**
- “Whenever I hear it, I remember...” — connect a trigger and memory
- “It reminds me of...” — describe an association

**3. Food you could eat every week**
- “I never get tired of...” — express a lasting preference
- “The best thing about it is...” — support the preference

**4. Planned travel or spontaneous travel?**
- “I tend to plan...” — describe a tendency
- “It depends on...” — make the answer conditional

**5. A hobby more people should try**
- “The good thing about it is...” — explain a benefit
- “It may seem..., but...” — correct an assumption

**6. Books, videos, or podcasts for learning?**
- “I learn best when...” — state a learning condition
- “Compared with..., ... is more...” — compare formats

## Relationships & Feelings · 6 topics

**1. What makes someone a good friend?**
- “A good friend is someone who...” — define through a relative clause
- “For example, ...” — support with evidence

**2. First impressions: useful or unfair?**
- “My first impression was..., but...” — revise a judgment
- “I don’t think you can tell... just from...” — limit a claim

**3. How do you handle conflict?**
- “I usually try to...” — describe a strategy
- “The important thing is to...” — state a principle

**4. Is it easy for you to ask for help?**
- “I find it easy/difficult to...” — evaluate a personal action
- “I’m more likely to ask when...” — describe a condition

**5. Time alone or time with people?**
- “I need both, but...” — give a balanced answer
- “When I’ve had too much..., I...” — explain the effect

**6. The best advice you’ve received**
- “Someone once told me to...” — report advice
- “It helped me because...” — explain its impact

## Society, Work & the Future · 6 topics

**1. Remote work or office work?**
- “The biggest advantage is...” — prioritize a benefit
- “The downside is that...” — acknowledge a drawback

**2. What should AI do—and not do?**
- “AI is useful for..., but it shouldn’t...” — draw a boundary
- “As long as..., I think...” — state a condition for acceptance

**3. Is convenience worth the environmental cost?**
- “We could reduce... by...” — propose a practical action
- “The problem is that...” — identify a constraint

**4. What makes a job meaningful?**
- “A job feels meaningful when...” — state a condition
- “Even if the pay is..., ...” — concede and contrast

**5. One skill people will need in ten years**
- “I think ... will become more important.” — make a prediction
- “That’s because...” — justify the prediction

**6. What would improve your city?**
- “I wish the city had more...” — express a present wish
- “If the city invested in..., it would...” — predict a hypothetical result

---

## Follow-up ladder for every topic

1. **Fact:** What/when/where?
2. **Reason:** Why? What makes you say that?
3. **Example:** Can you give me an example?
4. **Contrast:** Is that always true? What about the other side?
5. **Hypothetical:** What would change your mind?
6. **Return:** Has your answer changed since the start?

New topics should earn their place by creating genuine curiosity, not by providing a thin excuse to drill a grammar label.
